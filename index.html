Keynotes — the second verification layer

One word doesn't tell the whole story. The tool reads a drawing in layers: the term you type, the phrases around it, and — the reliable layer here — the keynotes, because keynotes are how elements are actually tagged. It's a second check on the term and phrase mapping, not a replacement for reading the rest of the set.

Layer 1
Term & alias
The curated word or abbreviation → its section.
Layer 2
Phrase & structure
Multi-word callouts, schedules, legends — weighted higher.
Layer 3 · verify
Keynote agreement
How the element was actually tagged — the confirmation.

Built from the projects — no master needed

There is no master keynote file. The standard is assembled from the projects themselves: mine each set's keynotes, line up how the same element was tagged across six buildings, and the most consistent, clearest wording becomes the proposed standard.

"This keynote could be better"

Because the tool sees every project, it can flag where a keynote you used is vaguer or less consistent than how another project tagged the same thing — and suggest the stronger wording.

How to use Spec Finder

A curated bridge from what's drawn to what must be specified — built from six real DIALOG projects.

1

Search a drawing term

Type any material, phrase, or abbreviation. Results are MasterFormat sections, ranked, each edged by its division colour.

2

Read the badge

required means a curated term reaches it — the tool can assert it. suggestion means a title-text match only, shown but never asserted. No match is an honest blank, not a guess.

3

Use the makers & product

Each card carries the acceptable manufacturers and the specified basis-of-design product, pulled from the real issued spec books.

4

Scan a whole set

On Scan drawings, drop the architectural PDF. It reads every sheet in your browser and summarises the required sections by division.

5

Open any section for the full picture

Click a result to see every drawing term, manufacturer, and product for it, which of the six projects issued it, and the sections it's usually issued alongside.

6

Filter by division & confidence

Colour filters narrow to the divisions you care about; the confidence filter (2+/3+/4+) shows only mappings that many projects agree on.

7

Flag what's wrong, export it back

See a bad mapping? Open the section and flag it — it saves in your browser only. Export the flags and hand them to a curation session to bake the fixes in.

The one rule: a section is only ever badged required when the curated library backs it. Reach without proof is just a guess — and this tool won't make one.
